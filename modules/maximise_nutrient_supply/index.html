    Maximise nutrient supply

    The maximise_nutrient_supply module contains helper functions for changing the optimisation objective of a GeoDistributor or FeedDistributor from the default (minimise deviation of crop areas and animal numbers from x0) to instead maximise the domestic supply of a selected nutrient (e.g. protein) from a chosen set of crop and/or animal products. All non-selected products remain constrained to exactly meet their baseline demand.

    The functions are accessed via cm.maximise_nutrient_supply and are called in a specific order relative to the .make(), .solve() and .apply_solution() methods of the distributor, as shown in the flowchart below.

    A typical use looks like this:

    feeddist.make()

cm.maximise_nutrient_supply.change_objective(
    feeddist,
    nutrient='protein',
    crop_prods=['food wheat', 'peas', 'broad beans'],
    animal_prods=[('cattle', 'milk'), ('cattle', 'meat')]
)
# Optionally require at least 0.5 kg fat per kg protein from the selected products
cm.maximise_nutrient_supply.constrain_nutrient(feeddist, 'fat', 0.5, '>=')

feeddist.solve(apply_solution=False)
cm.maximise_nutrient_supply.update_demand(feeddist, mode='trade')
feeddist.apply_solution()

cm.maximise_nutrient_supply.store_objective_contributions(feeddist)

    Note

    update_demand() should be run before .apply_solution(), since .apply_solution() allocates crop production between uses based on crop_prod_demand, which would otherwise still reflect the pre-optimisation demand. It must also be run before ByProductMgmt.calculate().

    Changing the objective

    maximise_nutrient_supply.change_objective()

    change_objective() modifies the distributor in place after .make() has built all constraints. It does four things:

    1. Relaxes constraint C1 for the selected products. C1 normally requires that production minus feed use exactly equals demand (food + non-food + export) for every crop and animal product. For the selected products this is changed to an inequality where only the food part of demand is relaxed:

      \[ \text{production} - \text{feed use} \geq \text{non-food demand} + \text{export demand} \]

      Production of the selected products can therefore move freely (up or down) away from the original food demand, while non-food and export demand are still guaranteed to be met. All non-selected products keep their original equality constraint.

    2. Rebuilds constraints C10 and C15 (by-product balances, FeedDistributor only). These constraints credit by-products generated per unit of production using ratios that are averaged over all demand routes (food, non-food and export). Without adjustment, extra production of a selected product would credit by-products (and hence feed availability) that the extra production would not actually generate. The by-product supply is therefore split into a fixed part for the baseline non-food and export demand and a part for the food route that scales with the relaxed production.

    3. Builds a nutrient mapper restricted to the selected products using nutrient_supply_from_x().

    4. Replaces the objective with one that maximises the mapped nutrient supply, subject to the same constraints as before (with C1, C10 and C15 modified as above):

      \[ \max \; \frac{\sum m_{crp} \, x_{crp} + \sum m_{ani} \, x_{ani} + \sum m_{fds} \, x_{fds}}{M} \;-\; \text{regularisation} \]

      where \(m\) are the per-unit nutrient coefficients from the mapper (\(m_{fds}\) is negative, see below), and \(M\) is the maximum absolute coefficient across the active variables (or 1 if all coefficients are zero). Scaling the nutrient reward improves numerical conditioning; the ridge weight acts relative to this scaled reward, rather than directly in kg or kcal.

      The single regularisation term is a quadratic ridge penalty with weight lambda_ridge (default 1e-9):

      \[ \text{regularisation} = \lambda_{ridge} \sum_{i \in \{\text{crops, animals}\}} \left[s_i (x_i - x_{0,i})\right]^2 \]

      Here \(s_i\) is the corresponding dist.scale_f weight. The penalty applies to all crop areas and animal numbers, whether or not they contribute to the nutrient objective. It favours the initial distribution x0 and adds curvature that can improve numerical stability. Feed quantities (x_fds) are excluded because their x0 values are zero placeholders rather than an initial ration.

      Set lambda_ridge=0 to disable regularisation. Larger weights favour the initial distribution more strongly and may reduce nutrient supply; their influence on total supply and the solution mix should be checked for the model being solved.

      If the solver still has numerical problems, try scale_constraints=True (rescales each constraint row without changing the feasible region) before increasing lambda_ridge.

    Other constraints (land use, rotations, sub-system shares, feed rations etc.) are left unchanged. These either limit x directly or are only relaxed further by increased production.

Docstring:
Changes a GeoDistributor/FeedDistributor's optimisation problem (in place) from
the default objective (minimising deviation of crop areas/animal numbers from x0)
to instead MAXIMISE the total domestic, edible supply of 'nutrient' from a
selected set of crop products ('crop_prods') and/or animal products
('animal_prods'). All other (non-selected) products remain constrained to exactly
meet their baseline demand, as in the default model.

Must be called after 'dist.make(...)' and before 'dist.solve()'. After solving,
call 'update_demand()' to keep 'dist.demand' consistent with the optimised
production.

What this does, step by step
-----------------------------
1. Relaxes constraint C1 for the selected products only (see
   '_relax_C1_for_selected_products()'). C1 normally reads
   '[production] - [feed use] == [demand]' (where [demand] = food + non-food +
   export demand) for every crop/animal product. For the selected crop_prods/
   animal_prods this is changed to
   '[production] - [feed use] >= [non-food demand] + [export demand]', i.e. only
   the FOOD portion of demand is relaxed: production must still (at minimum)
   cover whatever of it is used as feed plus the original non-food and export
   demand, but is otherwise free to move -- up or down -- away from the original
   FOOD demand figure, so that the optimiser can trade off between selected
   products based on nutrient supply and the ridge penalty. Every
   other (non-selected) product keeps its original equality constraint, i.e.
   keeps exactly meeting demand (food + non-food + export) as before.
2. Rebuilds constraints 'C10'/'C15' (if present -- FeedDistributor only; see
   '_split_byprod_supply_for_selected_products()') so that the by-product
   supply they credit to a selected product is split the same way: a
   food-route part that moves with the now-relaxed production, and a fixed
   non-food+export-route part frozen at the baseline floor. This is needed
   because C10/C15 apply 'DemandAndConversions.by_prod_per_crop_prod'/
   'by_prod_per_animal_prod' -- ratios BLENDED across a product's demand
   routes -- directly to x_ani/x_crp, not to a fixed demand figure like C1; see
   that function for why the unmodified constraints would otherwise let
   production growth credit by-products (and hence feed availability) that the
   extra production was never actually going to generate.
3. Builds a nutrient-supply mapper restricted to the selected products (see
   'nutrient_supply_from_x()').
4. Replaces 'dist.problem' with a new cvxpy.Problem that maximises the mapped
   scaled nutrient supply (implemented as minimising its negative), subject to
   the constraints built by 'dist.make()' (with C1, C10 and C15 modified per steps
   1-2), with one quadratic ridge penalty on scaled deviations of all crop
   areas and animal numbers from 'dist.x0'. Feed quantities are excluded.
   The penalty favours the initial distribution and can improve numerical
   stability; its weight also affects the trade-off with nutrient supply.

The remaining constraints retain their definitions:
- C2 (regional feed demand share), C16 (crop residue balance): inequalities
  relative to actual production with no equivalent blended-ratio dependency, so
  extra production only relaxes them further, never breaks them.
- C3 (max land use), C5 (max crop share of a crop_prod), C6 (rotation shares):
  real physical limits on x_crp itself, correctly still enforced -- e.g. C3
  still caps how much *more* of a selected crop can physically be grown.
- C4 (animal sub-system shares), C11-C14 (feed rations/imports): relate to
  x_ani/x_fds directly and are unaffected by relaxing C1's link between crop/
  animal PRODUCTION and demand.

Note on interpretation: with C1 relaxed, any production beyond baseline FOOD
demand for the selected products has no explicit downstream disposition in the
model (i.e. it is not literally allocated to extra consumption, export, etc. by
other CIBUSmod modules) until 'update_demand()' is run after solving -- see that
function. Non-food and export demand, by contrast, is still guaranteed to be at
least met (it forms the floor of the relaxed constraint), so it does not need
this treatment.

Parameters
----------
dist : GeoDistributor or FeedDistributor object
    Must already have been through 'dist.make(...)' (including constraint 1).
    Modified in place: 'dist.constraints' (C1 replaced) and 'dist.problem'
    (replaced with the new objective).
nutrient : str
    Nutrient to maximise supply of, see 'nutrient_supply_from_x()'.
crop_prods : list of str, default None
    crop_prod(s) to relax demand for and reward in the objective. At least one of
    'crop_prods'/'animal_prods' must be given.
animal_prods : list of (species, animal_prod) tuples, default None
    Analogous to 'crop_prods' but for animal products.
lambda_ridge : float, default 1e-8
    Weight of the quadratic penalty on deviations of all crop areas and animal
    numbers from 'dist.x0', weighted by 'dist.scale_f'. Feed quantities are
    excluded. The penalty is added to the negative nutrient reward after its
    coefficients are scaled to a maximum absolute value of 1. Larger weights
    favour the initial distribution and may reduce nutrient supply. Set to 0
    to disable the penalty.
scale_constraints : bool, default False
    If True, rescales every constraint row to a maximum absolute coefficient
    of 1 before building 'dist.problem' (see
    'CIBUSmod.optimisation.utils.scale_constraints_by_row()') -- a pure
    numerical-conditioning improvement (same feasible region, same optimum),
    not an approximation. Can help 'dist.solve()' avoid a generic SolverError
    (or an incorrect 'infeasible' report) on large/tightly-constrained
    problems. Try this before increasing 'lambda_ridge'. If False, constraint
    rows retain the scaling supplied by 'dist.make()'.

Returns
-------
dict of pandas.Series
    The nutrient-supply mapper actually used in the objective (i.e. already
    restricted to 'crop_prods'/'animal_prods'), with keys 'ani', 'crp' and (for a
    FeedDistributor) 'fds', indexed like 'dist.x_idx[...]'.

    maximise_nutrient_supply.constrain_nutrient()

    Maximising a single nutrient often gives a solution that is unbalanced in other respects, e.g. high in protein but with very little energy. constrain_nutrient() adds a constraint that the supply of another nutrient from the same selected products must be at least ('>=') or at most ('<=') a given factor times the supply of the maximised nutrient:

    \[ \text{supply}(\text{nutrient}) \;\; \geq \text{ or } \leq \;\; \text{factor} \times \text{supply}(\text{maximised nutrient}) \]

    Both supplies are calculated with the same type of mapper as the objective. factor is expressed in units of the constrained nutrient per unit of the maximised nutrient (kg/kg, or kcal/kg for energy). For example, after maximising protein:

    cm.maximise_nutrient_supply.constrain_nutrient(feeddist, 'fat', 0.5, '>=')     # at least 0.5 kg fat per kg protein
cm.maximise_nutrient_supply.constrain_nutrient(feeddist, 'kcal', 20_000, '<=') # at most 20,000 kcal per kg protein

    The constraint is implemented as a single row \((m_{\text{nutrient}} - \text{factor} \cdot m_{\text{obj}}) \, x \; \gtrless \; 0\), scaled to a maximum absolute coefficient of 1. The function can be called several times to constrain several nutrients, or to set both a minimum and a maximum for the same nutrient. Calling it again with the same nutrient and rel replaces the earlier constraint. The objective is rebuilt with the same settings as in change_objective(). All added constraints are removed when .make() is run again.

constrain_nutrient(dist, nutrient, factor, rel)

Docstring:
Adds a constraint to a GeoDistributor/FeedDistributor whose objective has been
changed by 'change_objective()', requiring that the supply of another 'nutrient'
from the optimised crop and animal products is at least ('>=') or at most ('<=')
'factor' times the supply of the nutrient being maximised:

    supply('nutrient')  factor * supply(nutrient maximised)

Both supplies are calculated with the same type of mapper and for the same
selection of crop_prods/animal_prods as the objective (see
'nutrient_supply_from_x()'), i.e. the domestic food supply from the optimised
products, net of their use as feed. E.g. after
'change_objective(dist, "protein", ...)',
'constrain_nutrient(dist, "fat", 0.5, ">=")' requires the optimised products to
supply at least 0.5 kg fat per kg protein, and
'constrain_nutrient(dist, "kcal", 20_000, "<=")' at most 20,000 kcal per kg
protein. 'factor' is in units of 'nutrient' per unit of the maximised nutrient
(kg/kg, or kcal/kg for energy).

Must be called after 'change_objective()' and before 'dist.solve()'. Can be
called several times to constrain several nutrients (or set both a minimum and
a maximum for one nutrient). Calling it again for the same 'nutrient' and 'rel'
replaces the earlier constraint, e.g. to change 'factor'. The constraints are
removed when 'dist.make()' is run again.

Implemented as the single-row constraint
'(mapper_nutrient - factor * mapper_objective) @ x  0', scaled to a
maximum absolute coefficient of 1 (which doesn't change the constraint, since the
right-hand side is 0), after which 'dist.problem' is rebuilt with the same
objective settings as in 'change_objective()'.

Parameters

    Must have been through 'change_objective()'.

    Nutrient to constrain, e.g. 'fat' or 'kcal' (see 'nutrient_supply_from_x()').
factor : float
    Supply of 'nutrient' per unit supply of the maximised nutrient to require.

    Whether the supply of 'nutrient' should be at least ('>=') or at most
    ('<=') 'factor' times the supply of the maximised nutrient.

Returns

    The mapper for 'nutrient' (see 'nutrient_supply_from_x()'), restricted to

    After solving

    maximise_nutrient_supply.update_demand()

    After solving, production of the selected products will usually differ from the demand stored in DemandAndConversions, and the difference has no explicit use (e.g. consumption or export) in the model. update_demand() re-makes the demand so that it is consistent with the optimised production, which is needed by modules that are calculated after solving (e.g. ByProductMgmt).

    1. The actual production minus feed use of each selected product is read off the relaxed C1 constraint at the solved x. This is the target that the re-made crop_prod_demand/animal_prod_demand must match.

    2. The gap between the target and current demand is allocated to food items whose main product (via conv_factor_main) is the selected product. How the gap is allocated depends on mode:

      mode Surplus production Production deficit
      'trade' (default) Consumption unchanged. Imports are first replaced by domestic production, then exports are added. Consumption unchanged. Domestic production is replaced by imports.
      'consumption' Domestic consumption is scaled up. Imported consumption is unchanged, so the import share decreases. Domestic consumption is scaled down. Imported consumption is unchanged.

      Food items made from a by-product of a selected product (e.g. butter from cream, a by-product of milk) are adjusted in proportion to how much of the by-product is generated. This is consistent with how their nutrients are credited in the objective.

    3. Waste, nutrient supply, product demand and by-products are re-calculated in DemandAndConversions from the new food_demand/export_demand.

    4. Steps 2–3 are repeated (up to max_iter times) until the re-made totals match the target within rtol/atol. Iteration is needed because the relation between food items and products is not fully linear, e.g. because of the dairy cream balance.

    Demand is re-made starting from the current food_demand and export_demand, so changes made to these after DemandAndConversions.calculate() (e.g. by induce_beef_exports()) are kept. A warning is issued if a gap cannot be fully allocated (e.g. a selected product with no directly mapped food item) or if demand for non-selected products changes as a side effect.

    Signature:
update_demand(dist, mode, max_iter, rtol, atol)

Docstring:
After 'dist.solve()', re-makes all data attributes of 'dist.demand' (a
DemandAndConversions object) so that they reflect the ACTUAL, optimised
production of whichever crop_prods/animal_prods 'change_objective()' relaxed
constraint C1 for, rather than the stale, pre-optimisation demand.

Why this is needed: 'change_objective()' relaxes C1 for the selected products
from '[production] - [feed use] == [demand]' to
'[production] - [feed use] >= [non-food demand] + [export demand]', so the
solved production for those products can end up above (or below) their original
demand. Nothing else in the model is told about that difference --
'dist.demand.data_attr' still holds the OLD figures, and the surplus (or
deficit) has no explicit disposition. Any module that runs after solving and
reads from 'dist.demand.data_attr' (chiefly 'ByProductMgmt', which reads
'by_products' and 'by_prod_demand') would otherwise work off stale figures.

What this does
--------------
1. Re-evaluates the SAME 'C1 (relaxed)' constraint matrix built by
   'change_objective()' at the solved 'dist.x', giving the actual
   '[production] - [feed use]' for each relaxed crop_prod/animal_prod row (so
   the selected products are read back off the constraint itself and need not
   be passed again here). This is the target that the re-made
   'crop_prod_demand'/'animal_prod_demand' totals must match.
2. Allocates the gap between that target and the current demand total to the
   food items whose main product (via 'conv_factor_main') is the relaxed
   crop_prod/animal_prod, by changing 'food_demand' and/or 'export_demand'
   according to 'mode':
   - 'consumption': the DOMESTIC part of consumption of those food items is
     scaled up or down (uniformly per product) to match production, while
     imported consumption is kept as is, implicitly changing import shares.
     E.g. 10 kg consumption at 50% import share with production increased
     from 5 to 10 kg gives 15 kg consumption (10 kg domestic + 5 kg
     imported, i.e. 33% import share); with production reduced to zero, 5 kg
     of imported consumption remains. Food items that are 100% imported get
     domestic consumption added in proportion to their imported consumption
     if production increases. Existing exports are left unchanged.
   - 'trade': consumption is left unchanged. If production exceeds demand,
     imports of those food items are first replaced by domestic production
     and, if that is not enough, exports of the same food items are
     introduced (pro rata to their consumption). If production falls short
     of demand, imports are increased (domestic consumption is replaced by
     imports).
   Food items supplied from a by-product generated by a relaxed product (i.e.
   whose main product is a by_prod, e.g. butter from cream, a by-product of
   milk, or offals from edible offals) have their DOMESTIC consumption
   changed in proportion to the amount of the by-product generated (in the
   generating products' 'food' demand route, see '_by_prod_generation()'), so
   that the share of the generated by-product used for food stays as at
   baseline -- consistent with how 'change_objective()' credits their
   nutrients (see '_by_prod_nutrient_credit()'). In 'consumption' mode
   imported consumption is kept as is; in 'trade' mode total consumption is
   kept, so that domestic consumption replaces (or is replaced by) imports,
   and any domestic supply beyond total consumption is exported. This also
   matters for demand itself, via the dairy "cream balance": cream demand
   that isn't met by cream generated from other dairy foods induces exports
   of milk powder (and hence demand for milk), so leaving e.g. butter
   consumption unchanged when demand for milk is reduced would induce large
   exports that prevent demand from matching production.
3. Re-runs 'calculate_waste()', 'calculate_nutrient_supply()' and
   'calculate_product_demand_and_by_products()', so that every data attribute
   derived from 'food_demand'/'export_demand' ('food_demand_to_processing',
   'waste', 'nutrient_supply', 'crop_prod_demand', 'animal_prod_demand',
   'crop_resid_demand', 'by_prod_demand', 'by_products' and the
   'by_prod_per_*' ratios) is consistent with the new demand. Since the surplus/
   deficit is routed through actual food items, by-products are generated by
   those food items' own conversion routes.
4. Repeats steps 2-3 until the re-made totals match the target (within
   'rtol'), since the mapping from food items to products is not exactly
   linear: compound foods (recipes) are never adjusted (adjusting them would
   also change demand for their other ingredients), and the dairy "cream
   balance" (see '_fix_cream_balance()' in DemandAndConversions) may induce
   further exports when dairy demand changes. Each iteration normally closes
   the remaining gap almost entirely.

The re-make starts from the CURRENT 'food_demand'/'export_demand' rather than
re-calculating them from parameters, so that changes made to them after
'DemandAndConversions.calculate()' (e.g. by
'CIBUSmod.utils.helpers.induce_beef_exports()') are retained. Changes made
directly to downstream attributes (e.g. editing 'crop_prod_demand' in place)
are, however, overwritten by step 3 -- a warning is issued if the re-made
demand (before any adjustment) does not reproduce the demand that 'dist' was
built with.

Fallbacks and limitations (each results in a warning):
- 'consumption' mode, where a relaxed product with surplus production has no
  consumption (domestic or imported) of any directly mapped food item to scale
  up (e.g. it is only consumed via compound foods): the surplus is instead
  exported as in 'trade' mode.
- A relaxed product with no food item mapped to it via 'conv_factor_main' at
  all, or a deficit that cannot be covered (e.g. all of its directly mapped
  domestic consumption already replaced/removed), cannot be fully allocated
  and its re-made total will not match the target.
- Demand for non-relaxed products may change if a relaxed product affects
  them via the cream balance or a compound food. A warning lists any
  non-relaxed C1 rows whose re-made total no longer matches what 'dist' was
  solved with.

Must be called after 'dist.solve()', and before anything that consumes
'dist.demand.data_attr' is calculated. Only the solved 'dist.x' is used, so
'dist.apply_solution()' is NOT required beforehand -- and should preferably be
run AFTER this function (i.e. 'dist.solve(apply_solution=False)', then
'update_demand()', then 'dist.apply_solution()', or alternatively re-make and
re-solve 'dist' with the default objective), since
'allocate_crop_production_per_use()' (called by 'dist.apply_solution()') splits
crop production between uses based on 'crop_prod_demand', which would otherwise
still be the stale, pre-optimisation demand. It must also be called before
'ByProductMgmt.calculate()', which aborts (with a warning) if run more than once
per 'DemandAndConversions.calculate()' -- see its docstring.

    maximise_nutrient_supply.store_objective_contributions()

    store_objective_contributions() calculates how much each animal and crop contributes to the objective (nutrient coefficient × x) at the current solution. The results are stored as data attributes on the distributor, so that they are saved with the scenario when session.store() is called:

    • nutrient_contrib_animals: \(m_{ani} \cdot x_{ani}\), indexed like x_animals.
    • nutrient_contrib_crops: \(m_{crp} \cdot x_{crp}\), indexed like x_crops, net of feed use. Feed use is only known nationally per crop product, so the (negative) feed term is allocated to the crops and regions producing that crop product in proportion to their contribution.

    The sum of both attributes equals the unscaled nutrient supply from the mapper, in kg or kcal, before regularisation. The solver minimises the negative of this supply divided by \(M\), plus the regularisation penalties, so its objective value has a different sign and scale. The mapper from the most recent change_objective() call is used, but contributions are evaluated at whatever dist.x currently holds. The function can therefore be called either directly after solving or after update_demand() followed by a re-make and re-solve with the default objective.

    Warning

    dist.data_attr persists across scenarios. If a later scenario does not call store_objective_contributions(), the attributes from the previous scenario are stored again unless removed, e.g. with dist.data_attr.remove('nutrient_contrib_animals').

Docstring:
Stores each animal's and crop's contribution to the objective set by
'change_objective()' (i.e. mapper * x at the current solution 'dist.x') as data
attributes on 'dist':
- 'nutrient_contrib_animals': mapper['ani'] * x_ani, indexed like 'x_animals'.
- 'nutrient_contrib_crops': mapper['crp'] * x_crp, indexed like 'x_crops', but
  NET of the (negative) mapper['fds'] * x_fds term for feed use of the selected
  crop_prods. Feed use is only known per crop_prod nationally (not per crop or
  region it was grown in), so for each (prod_system, crop_prod) the total feed
  term is allocated over the crops/regions producing that crop_prod pro rata to
  their (positive) contribution via that crop_prod. I.e. each crop's
  contribution via a crop_prod is scaled by
  (production term + feed term) / production term for that crop_prod.

Summing both attributes gives the unscaled nutrient supply, sum(mapper * x)
over x_ani, x_crp and x_fds, in kg or kcal before the ridge penalty. The solver
minimises the negative scaled supply plus the penalty, so its objective value
has a different sign and scale. If a selected crop_prod is used as feed but
not produced from any x_crp, its feed term cannot be allocated to a crop and
a warning is issued.

The nutrient mapper used is the one built by the most recent call to
'change_objective(dist, ...)', whereas the contributions are evaluated at
whatever 'dist.x' currently holds. This function can therefore be called either
right after solving the maximise-nutrient problem, or after 'update_demand()'
followed by re-making and re-solving 'dist' with the default objective (in
which case the contributions refer to that final solution). Call it before
'session.store()' for the attributes to be stored with the scenario.

Note that 'dist.data_attr' persists across scenarios (as do the attributes
stored here), so for a scenario run after one that called this function but
that does not call it itself, the previous scenario's contributions would be
stored too unless removed (e.g. 'dist.data_attr.remove(
'nutrient_contrib_animals')').

    Nutrient mapping

    maximise_nutrient_supply.nutrient_supply_from_x()

    nutrient_supply_from_x() is the underlying function that translates the optimisation variables into dietary nutrient supply. It returns per-unit coefficients, i.e. how much of the nutrient one hectare of a crop, one animal or one unit of feed is worth, as a dict of Series with keys 'crp', 'ani' and (for FeedDistributor) 'fds'. The nutrient supply for a given solution is then

    \[ \sum m_{crp} \, x_{crp} + \sum m_{ani} \, x_{ani} + \sum m_{fds} \, x_{fds} \]

    The coefficients are calculated as follows:

    • Crops and animals (x_crp, x_ani) are mapped to the products they produce (via CropProduction.production and AnimalHerd.production) and get positive coefficients.
    • Feeds (x_fds) are mapped to the crop products they use (via FeedMgmt.par.feed_to_prod) and get negative coefficients, representing crop production used as feed instead of food.
    • The nutrient content per kg of product is based on composition, conv_factor_main, waste_share and recipe in DemandAndConversions. When a product is used for several food items, these are weighted by their share of the product's food demand.
    • Non-food and export demand are deliberately ignored. A product's whole production is credited with its full food-nutrient density, so the mapper represents the potential food-nutrient supply from more production, not the baseline average. As a result, the mapper does not sum to DemandAndConversions.nutrient_supply for products that have non-food or export demand.
    • Nutrients from food items made from by-products (e.g. butter from cream) are credited to the products generating the by-product. Only the share of the by-product used for food at baseline is credited.

    The function can also be used on its own, e.g. for reporting how much of a nutrient comes from each crop or animal in a solved model. It should be called right after .make() and before .solve(): if a previous solution has scaled a herd to zero animals in a region, the per-animal production cannot be recovered and a warning is raised.

nutrient_supply_from_x(

Docstring:
Builds a mapper from x_ani, x_crp and (if available) x_fds to the dietary
supply of a given 'nutrient', based on the 'composition', 'conv_factor_main' and
'waste_share' (and, where used, 'recipe') parameters in 'DemandAndConversions'.

This returns PER-UNIT coefficients (not the nutrient supply for any particular
x) -- i.e. how much 'nutrient' one unit of a given crop area/animal number/feed
amount is worth. To get the nutrient supply for a given x, take
sum(mapper['crp'] * x['crp']) + sum(mapper['ani'] * x['ani'])
+ sum(mapper['fds'] * x['fds']) (the last term already carries a negative sign,
see below).

Crop areas (x_crp) and animal numbers (x_ani) are mapped to the crop and animal
products they produce (via 'CropProduction.production' and 'AnimalHerd.production')
and get POSITIVE coefficients, representing crop/animal production that ends up
as food. Feed amounts (x_fds), if available, are mapped to the crop products they
consume (via the 'feed_to_prod' parameter in the 'FeedMgmt' module) and get
NEGATIVE coefficients, representing crop production diverted to feed rather than
food.

The per-unit nutrient content used for x_crp/x_ani/x_fds is the crop/animal
product's POTENTIAL food-nutrient density -- i.e. based ONLY on
DemandAndConversions.crop_prod_demand/animal_prod_demand's 'food' column, ignoring
'non-food'/'export' entirely (see '_nutrient_supply_factors()'). This means the
mapper deliberately does NOT net out to DemandAndConversions.nutrient_supply when
summed over a feasible x in general -- it only does so for a crop_prod/animal_prod
with no non-food/export demand at baseline. Where a crop_prod/animal_prod DOES
have non-food/export demand, x_crp/x_ani's production credits the FULL
food-nutrient density to the ENTIRE quantity produced (including the portion that,
at baseline, actually goes to non-food/export), which is what makes the mapper
suitable for 'change_objective()': it answers "how much MORE food-nutrient supply
is achievable from this product", not "how much does the baseline mix actually
deliver on average". x_fds is still subtracted using the same per-unit figure, so
feed use is still correctly netted out of whatever x_crp/x_ani credited -- this
exclusion only concerns the non-food/export split, not the feed split.

Must be run after GeoDistributor.make() (so that geodist.x_idx is defined).
CropProduction.production/AnimalHerd.production are re-scaled in place by
GeoDistributor.apply_solution() (the default when calling .solve()). The crop
part of the mapper is computed directly from CropProduction's parameters and so
is unaffected by this either way. The animal part is derived from AnimalHerd's
current data (there being no similarly simple parameter-only route to herd
production), which is safe UNLESS a prior .solve() has already scaled some herd
to 0 animals in a given region -- in that case the corresponding per-animal
production rate is unrecoverable and a warning is raised. To avoid this, call
this function right after .make(), before .solve().
